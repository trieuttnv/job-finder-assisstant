## Cross-cutting constraints

These bind every task.

1. **No invention.** "The assistant must not invent qualifications, employers, projects, or achievements. A system that fabricates a role in the financial industry is a failure."
2. **Traceability.** "Show us how you hold that line in your own design": every stated fact must be traceable to a source. (The brief's sentence reads "nobody can trace the model's output back to a source"; we read it as the failure to prevent, see Open Questions.)
3. **Subject is the author only.** "The subject is you, nothing resembling unpaid work for us." "Self-contained: no other systems, no production data, and no internal access."
4. **Stack is free but must be justified.** "The reasonable choice scores no better than a justified one."
5. **Reasoning is graded.** "Depth beats length. Every section is graded on your reasoning as much as on what runs."
6. **Env-only config, no secrets.** "Take its configuration from environment variables and ship an `.env.example`." "Please don't commit a real key."
7. **Commit only what you'd show.** "Commit only what you're comfortable having us read." Redacted and synthetic content is labelled.
8. **Runnable by a stranger.** "Another engineer should be able to understand and run your project from it with no further guidance."
9. **Honest results.** Results are committed with failures; "it seemed to work when I tried it" is not evidence.
10. **Disclosed reuse and real history.** "Reuse is fine; undisclosed reuse is not." Work lands as a sequence of meaningful commits.

**Time.** The brief mentions a "time box" (Task 5) but states no limit. This design has no timebox; the time actually spent is reported.
